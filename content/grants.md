## Investing in research that matters

The Fund is being developed to provide competitive research grants to eligible master’s and PhD students enrolled at recognised universities in Africa. Support is intended for research that addresses important challenges in agriculture, food systems, rural livelihoods and natural-resource management.

### Research areas

We welcome research ideas across agricultural and related disciplines, including:

- Soil health, soil fertility and nutrient management.
- Crop science, agronomy, plant breeding and crop protection.
- Climate change adaptation, mitigation and climate-resilient agriculture.
- Water management, irrigation and sustainable land management.
- Livestock systems, animal health and integrated crop–livestock systems.
- Agricultural engineering, mechanisation and digital agriculture.
- Agricultural economics, markets, value chains and rural livelihoods.
- Food systems, nutrition-sensitive agriculture and post-harvest management.
- Biodiversity, agroecology, underutilised crops and natural-resource management.

Research should demonstrate scientific merit, relevance to African contexts and a clear rationale for the proposed work.

### Eligible research costs

Subject to future grant guidelines and available funding, eligible costs may include:

- Field experiments and on-farm research.
- Laboratory analyses and diagnostic services.
- Data collection, surveys and enumerator costs.
- Research materials, consumables and specialised supplies.
- Research-related travel and field logistics.
- Dissemination of findings and stakeholder engagement.

### How applications will be assessed

The Fund intends to use a transparent and competitive review process. Proposed assessment considerations include:

1. Relevance to agricultural and development priorities in Africa.
2. Scientific quality, clarity of objectives and research design.
3. Feasibility of the work plan and proposed budget.
4. Potential contribution to knowledge and practical outcomes.
5. Applicant’s preparedness and institutional research environment.
6. Potential for mentorship, collaboration and dissemination.
