## Why the Fund is needed

Agriculture remains central to food security, employment and economic development across Africa. The continent also faces interconnected challenges, including declining soil fertility, climate variability, land degradation, emerging pests and diseases, weak input and output markets, and limited access to appropriate technologies. Addressing these challenges requires locally grounded research conducted by scientists who understand the farming systems, institutions and communities in which agricultural development takes place.

African universities train many talented master’s and PhD students capable of generating locally relevant agricultural knowledge. However, a considerable gap remains between the research funding that may be available and the ability of African postgraduate students to identify, access and successfully compete for it. Awareness of existing opportunities is often limited, while complex application procedures, limited grant-writing experience and institutional eligibility requirements can place students at a disadvantage.

Consequently, many postgraduate students struggle to finance experiments and disseminate their findings. Some are forced to compromise the quality and reliability of their research; others delay, narrow or abandon their work because they cannot meet essential costs. Important country- and Africa-specific agricultural challenges can also receive limited research attention despite their direct relevance to farmers and national food security.

## Our aim

> To strengthen the agricultural research capacity of African postgraduate scholars studying at African universities by providing accessible research funding and mentorship.

## Our objectives

The African Agricultural Scholars Research Fund aims to:

1. Provide competitive research grants to eligible master’s and PhD students enrolled at recognised universities in Africa.
2. Support locally relevant research addressing priority challenges affecting African agriculture, food systems, rural livelihoods and natural-resource management.
3. Enable students to meet essential research costs, including fieldwork, laboratory analysis, data collection, specialised research materials and dissemination of findings.
4. Connect funded scholars with experienced scientists who can strengthen research design, data analysis, scientific writing and professional development.
5. Promote collaboration and knowledge exchange among African students, universities, research institutions, farmers, development organisations and private-sector partners.
6. Support communication and practical application of research findings through scientific publications, policy products, stakeholder engagement and farmer-oriented materials.

## Our intended outcome

The initiative aims to contribute to a new generation of well-supported African agricultural researchers who can produce credible, locally relevant evidence and translate it into solutions for farmers, institutions and policymakers across the continent.

## Our approach

The Fund intends to communicate opportunities openly, provide an accessible application process and strengthen applicants’ ability to develop competitive research proposals. Grant decisions are intended to be guided by transparent calls for proposals, independent scientific review and clearly defined selection criteria.

Equitable participation across countries, institutions, disciplines and genders—including scholars based at universities with limited research resources—is an important consideration in the initiative’s development.

## Governance and accountability

The Fund will seek contributions from individuals, alumni associations, research institutions, foundations, development partners, private companies and other organisations committed to strengthening agricultural research capacity in Africa. Appropriate governance, financial management and accountability systems are to be established to support responsible use of resources and transparent, scientifically credible grant decisions.

*The Fund is an initiative in development. Details of its legal structure, governance arrangements, grant availability and operating procedures will be published as they are formally established.*
