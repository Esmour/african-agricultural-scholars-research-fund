      <h2>Dr. Kamaluddin Tijjani Aliyu</h2>
      <p class="role">Cropping Systems Agronomist, CIMMYT</p>
      <p>Dr. Kamaluddin is a cropping systems agronomist with over 13 years of research experience across diverse tropical agricultural systems. He holds a PhD in Agronomy, specializing in crops and cropping systems in dryland environments, from Bayero University, Kano, Nigeria. His research focuses on cropping systems, soil health and fertility management, plant nutrition, sustainable agricultural intensification, and digital agricultural advisory services. He has built his research career within the CGIAR system, spending eight years at the International Institute of Tropical Agriculture (IITA) before joining the International Maize and Wheat Improvement Center (CIMMYT), where he currently works. His research spans West, East and Southern Africa, contributing to the development and promotion of sustainable cropping systems, soil health management. His work also extends to digital agricultural innovation, including the development of decision-support applications for Malawi and Zambia that help match sustainable intensification practices to farmers' production environments, resource endowments and objectives.</p>

      <h2>Dr. Ismail Ibrahim Garba</h2>
      <p class="role">Farming Systems Scientist</p>
      <p>Dr. Ismail Ibrahim Garba is an agricultural systems scientist at CSIRO, Australia, working at the intersection of agronomy, farming systems analysis, crop modelling and data science. My research focuses on understanding how crop production systems respond to environmental conditions and management practices, and how this knowledge can be translated into practical insights for improving agricultural productivity, resilience and resource-use efficiency. I specialises in analysing genotype × environment × management (G × E × M) interactions, envirotyping and farming systems modelling to understand spatial and temporal variation in crop performance. I also develops computational and analytical frameworks that combine agronomic knowledge, data engineering and advanced statistical methods to support evidence-based agricultural decision-making for diverse farming environments across Africa and Australia.</p>

      <h2>Dr. Tukur Abdulazeez</h2>
      <p class="role">Research Scientist</p>
      <p>Dr. Tukur Abdulazeez Tukur is an agronomist and multidisciplinary agricultural scientist combining expertise in agronomy, environmental science, remote sensing, precision agriculture, and machine learning, with over 12 years of research and development experience in agricultural research for development. His research interests span precision agriculture, crop nutrition and nutrient-use efficiency, hyperspectral sensing, digital agriculture, climate-smart agronomy, and the integration of remote sensing and machine learning to improve nutrient management, agricultural productivity, and environmental sustainability. He is completing a PhD in Geography at the University of Dundee, United Kingdom, where his research integrates hyperspectral sensing, field-based agronomic measurements, and machine learning to advance nitrogen diagnosis and management. Previously, he worked at the International Crops Research Institute for the Semi-Arid Tropics (ICRISAT), contributing to multi-location on-station and on-farm research-for-development activities focused on sustainable intensification, crop productivity, soil fertility management, climate resilience, and farmer-oriented agricultural interventions across Nigeria.</p>

      <h2>Dr. Adam M. Adam</h2>
      <p class="role">Soil Scientist and Cropping Systems Agronomist</p>
      <p>Dr. Adam Muhammad Adam is a soil scientist and cropping systems agronomist conducting research at the intersection of soil fertility, crop physiology, genetics, and agroecosystem modelling. His research interests include integrating genetic information into process-based crop growth simulation models, genotype-by-environment-by-management (G × E × M) interactions, rhizosphere processes, and building resilient tropical cropping systems. He joined the University of Göttingen as a Postdoctoral Researcher in the Tropical Plant Production and Agricultural Systems Modelling (TROPAGS) group, where his work focuses on linking genetic traits to crop growth models to better simulate plant responses to multiple concurrent abiotic and biotic stresses. Prior to joining the University of Göttingen, Adam conducted research at the Institute of Agricultural Sciences in the Tropics (Hans-Ruthenberg-Institute), University of Hohenheim, focusing on legume integration, intercropping dynamics, and landscape-scale crop modelling. He also worked in Nigeria at Bayero University, Kano.</p>

      <h2>Dr. Benjamin Kurya</h2>
      <p class="role">Plant breeder</p>
      <p>Suleman Benjamin Kurya is a plant breeder currently completing a PhD in Plant Genetics and Breeding at Adelaide University, Australia. His research focuses on the application of CRISPR/Cas9-mediated gene editing to introduce agronomically desirable traits in barley, with particular emphasis on herbicide tolerance and nitrogen use efficiency. He holds a Masters degree Plant Genetics and Breeding from the University of Western Australia and a Bachelor of Agricultural Technology in Crop Production Technology from Abubakar Tafawa Balewa University, Nigeria. He has over 14 years of professional experience spanning crop breeding, seed systems, agricultural research and development. Prior to pursuing his postgraduate studies in Australia, Suleman worked with the International Crops Research Institute for the Semi-Arid Tropics (ICRISAT), where he contributed to groundnut breeding and the release of three improved varieties in Nigeria. His industry experience also covers seed production and certification, out-grower coordination, agricultural project management and business development. Looking ahead, Suleman is interested in advancing his research in precision breeding and crop adaptation to environmental stresses. He is interested in applying advances in plant science to address practical agricultural challenges to promote agriculture in Africa and the world.</p>
